# Reply Handling & Discovery-Call Playbook (Charm Offensive)

Adapt to the user's voice and the specific reply. Keep the tone from `voice-and-humour.md`.

---

## Response templates

### Positive reply → book the call
> Hey {name},
> Thank you for replying!
> I'd love to chat sometime! Could you let me know when works for you?
> Alternatively, book in some time here: {booking link}
> I'm looking forward to talking to you! :)
> Thanks so much,
> {your name}

*(If they asked specific questions, answer them warmly above this.)*

### "Tell me more" → brief intro + booking link
> Hey {name},
> Thank you for replying! Here's a brief introduction to {company}. I'll be brief and use bullet points:
> - We are {what you do — you can slip in one light humour beat}.
> - We're based in {X} and have {X} wonderful staff.
> - We help you {solve problem} without {pain point you avoid}. You can see how we {result} for
>   {client} by {clicking here / link}.
> I'd love a 10-minute chat. Could you let me know when works for you? Or book here: {booking link}
> Thank you so much for your time.
> Cheers,
> {your name}
> {serious / descriptive / funny job titles}

### "Not right now" → keep the door open (pick what fits)
- "May I contact you in 3/6/9 months to see if anything's changed? I promise to include
  {something cute/amusing/interesting}." → set a CRM + diary reminder.
- "Mind if I add you to our newsletter? It contains {X} and I think you'd find it useful because {X}.
  If not, no worries of course! Don't ask — don't get! :)"
- LinkedIn invite: "Hey {name}, thanks for replying to my email. I'd love to connect with you on
  LinkedIn, if you don't mind. Thank you, {your name}."

### Negative reply
- If cold emailing: make sure they're unsubscribed from future mailings.
- If they're rude: don't reply.
- If polite: "Apologies, {name}. I won't contact you again. Best of luck with everything. Have a
  great week, {your name}."
- Don't be discouraged — everyone gets these. If you're *only* getting negatives, look at your data,
  copy, and offer.

---

## Before the call
- Review their website, socials, any news, and their LinkedIn (background, recommendations, common
  ground). Know enough not to make silly mistakes — remember it won't be a perfect picture.
- For bigger clients, offer/accept an NDA; it's not required but pre-empting it lets you ask more.

## On the call — keep THEM talking (question bank)
- **Permission to record:** "Do you mind if I record this? My handwriting is like a toddler's! If
  not, no worries of course."
- **Get them to introduce themselves:** owners → "I've looked at your site, but I'd love to hear
  directly — mind giving me the elevator pitch of what you do and how you got started?" Others →
  "I'd love to hear more about {X}. What's it like to work there? What are your main responsibilities?"
- **Goals/ambitions:** "What are your goals this year?" / "What projects must you complete this
  quarter/year?" / "What's your 'picture of success'?"
- **Past activity:** "What {relevant activity} are you currently doing?" / "How did that work?" /
  "Do you work with any {suppliers/agencies} now — or have before? How was that?"
- **Constraints:** "Do you have any brand guidelines / policy documents I should know about?"
- **Soft pitch (get a 'yes' — make it a no-brainer):** "Just thinking out loud — have you considered
  {approach}?" → "We did {X} for {client} and they saw {result}. I can see it working for you. Would
  you consider it?" → "From what you've said, we could review your current activity and give you a
  mini-audit — free of charge." / "We can brainstorm a few ideas for you and send them over, if you'd
  like?"
- **KPIs:** dept heads → "What is your success judged on? What are your KPIs?" Founders → "What
  metrics matter to you? What do you look for in reports?"
- **Budget:** "Do you have a budget assigned, even ballpark? It helps me make the proposal realistic."
  If they won't say, offer a **tiered proposal** (a few levels of service/price).
- **Decision-makers:** "I hope you don't mind me asking — alas, it's my job — are there other decision
  makers I need to impress?"
- **How do I impress you:** "This may sound cheeky, but… how do I impress you? Any areas of the
  proposal I should focus on most?"
- **Propose the proposal + set a deadline + book the next call:** "I'd love to send you a short
  proposal with thoughts on how we could help you {solve problem}. Mind if I put a short deck/document
  together?" → "I can get it to you by {Friday}. Would you mind if we set up a 15-minute call to run
  through it next week?" Offer to present in person for local/large/important prospects. Then ask for
  target customer profiles / top-line analytics to sharpen the proposal ("I understand if that's too
  sensitive at this stage").

## After the call — the follow-up email
Send promptly. Include: their upcoming projects, goals, challenges (set the scene); a brief outline of
how you can help (link case studies); what will be in the proposal; when they'll get it; and, if
booked, "I'm looking forward to catching up on {date/time}." Move the deal to "Qualified" in the CRM.
Then hand off to `charm-proposal`.
