# 02 — Funding Products (plain-English cards)

Purpose: give the Funding Affiliate Manager agent enough plain-English
understanding of each product family to talk to affiliates about *what kind
of client fits what kind of product* — not to quote rates, approval odds, or
terms. **This agent never underwrites or quotes.** For any specific number a
prospect or affiliate asks about, the answer is: send them to
`{{AFFILIATE_LINK}}` / a Funding Advisor, don't quote a figure from memory.

Every card below is flagged by its source:
- **[7FF-LISTED]** — appears on 7 Figures Funding's own public marketplace
  page(s), so it's confirmed to be something 7FF actually offers today.
- **[INDUSTRY-STANDARD]** — a well-known business-funding product type from
  general industry knowledge and/or the funding-director-orgo product
  catalog, but **not confirmed** to be on 7FF's current public marketplace.

Two of 7FF's own pages disagree on the post-intro APR for 0% cards
(17.99–29.99% on `/business-funding` vs 15.99–24.99% on `/startup-funding`,
both retrieved 2026-09-23). This agent should never quote either number to an
affiliate or prospect — point them to the live marketplace page instead, and
flag the discrepancy to a human as a possible stale-page issue.

## [7FF-LISTED] 0% Introductory-Rate Business Credit Cards / "Credit Stacking"

**What it is:** Multiple business and/or personal credit cards, each with a
0% introductory APR period, used together ("stacked") to create working
capital. Cards may be issued to the business or the individual depending on
the issuer.

**Who it fits:** Owners with decent personal credit who want capital without
giving up equity or collateral, and who can manage several card accounts and
a 2–3% minimum monthly payment.

**Qualifying signals (qualitative only):** Personal credit generally
considered good-to-excellent by issuers; low existing card utilization;
comfortable with multiple hard inquiries across issuers.

**Red flags:** Recent bankruptcies, heavy existing card utilization, no plan
to pay down principal before the intro period ends, or a business that can't
service a 2–3%/month payment.

**7FF's own disclosure language to reuse (not paraphrase into something
stronger):** this is a *consumer credit product used for business purposes*
— the owner is personally liable, it may appear on their personal credit
report, and applying can trigger a hard inquiry. Introductory period,
go-to APR, and fees are set by the card issuer and vary by product.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "It's using a handful of 0%-intro
business cards together as working capital instead of a loan — no equity,
no collateral, but you're personally on the hook for the cards."

## [7FF-LISTED] Business Line of Credit

**What it is:** A revolving credit facility; the business draws what it
needs and (per 7FF's own page) has no payment obligation on undrawn funds,
though some lines carry maintenance/draw/unused-line fees.

**Who it fits:** Businesses with fluctuating or seasonal cash-flow needs that
want flexibility rather than a lump sum.

**Qualifying signals:** Established banking history, reasonably consistent
deposits, existing revenue.

**Red flags:** No business bank account history, inconsistent or declining
deposits, prior defaults on revolving credit.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "It's like a credit card for the
business — draw what you need, pay it back, draw again — instead of one big
loan."

---

## [7FF-LISTED] Business-Purpose Personal Term Loan

**What it is:** A personal installment loan used toward a business purpose
(often to consolidate debt or strengthen a personal financial position
before pursuing more business funding). 7FF is explicit this is a *consumer*
credit product even though the purpose is business.

**Who it fits:** Owners who need a fixed amount with a fixed payment and
whose personal credit profile qualifies, often as one leg of a larger
funding plan.

**Red flags:** High existing personal debt-to-income, recent late payments,
no clear plan for the proceeds.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "A personal loan the owner takes out
and puts into the business — same as any personal loan, just used for
business needs."

---

## [7FF-LISTED] Business Term Loan

**What it is:** A lump sum repaid over a fixed schedule; 7FF positions this
for "more established businesses" and "general working capital needs."

**Who it fits:** Businesses with enough time in business and revenue history
to support a longer amortization.

**Red flags:** Very early-stage business, thin or unverifiable revenue,
existing high-cost debt stacked on top of the business already.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "A traditional business loan — lump
sum now, fixed payments over a set number of years."

---

## [7FF-LISTED] Short-Term Business Loan (may be structured as an MCA)

**What it is:** Fast funding for near-term needs. 7FF's own page notes this
is "typically more expensive than alternative options" and that *some* of
these products are actually structured as **merchant cash advances (MCA)**
rather than a loan — meaning repayment is tied to future receivables/sales
rather than a fixed loan schedule. 7FF says the Funding Advisor identifies
the actual structure before the client proceeds.

**Who it fits:** Businesses that need cash fast and have consistent daily/
weekly card or bank deposits to support a receivables-based repayment if it
turns out to be structured as an MCA.

**Red flags:** Inconsistent daily deposits, already carrying one or more
active MCA positions ("stacked" MCAs), thin margins that can't absorb a
factor-rate cost of capital.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "Fast money for a near-term need —
just know it might come back as an advance against future sales rather than
a traditional loan, and it usually costs more for the speed."

---

## [7FF-LISTED] SBA Loan

**What it is:** 7FF helps prepare and submit SBA applications to
participating lenders. 7FF states plainly it is *not affiliated with,
endorsed by, or acting on behalf of* the U.S. Small Business Administration.

**Who it fits:** Established businesses (or well-prepared startups under
certain SBA programs) that can support a longer underwriting process and
want the SBA's typically longer terms.

**Red flags:** Business or owner ineligible under SBA rules (e.g., certain
industries, ownership/citizenship issues), inability to produce tax returns
and financial statements, need for money faster than SBA timelines allow.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "The government-backed loan program
— usually better terms, but a slower process and more paperwork."

## [7FF-LISTED] Business-Use HELOC

**What it is:** A home-equity line of credit where proceeds are used for the
business. 7FF's marketplace page shows this among the longest terms and
largest amounts of any product it lists.

**Who it fits:** Owners with meaningful equity in real estate who are
comfortable using that property as collateral for business capital.

**Red flags:** Little/no home equity, existing liens, owner unwilling to put
real estate at risk for business capital, non-owner-occupied complications.

**Compliance note:** the internal Funding Machine operating notes
(funding-director-orgo) stress that a HELOC "prescreen" is never an offer,
and eligibility/property/lien/occupancy/state confirmation happens in a
separate step. This agent should never tell an affiliate a HELOC referral is
pre-approved.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "Using home equity as the collateral
for business funding — bigger amounts, longer terms, but the house is on the
line."

## [INDUSTRY-STANDARD — not confirmed on 7FF's current public marketplace] Equipment Financing

**What it is:** A loan or lease secured by the equipment being purchased.

**Who it fits:** Businesses buying a specific, identifiable piece of
equipment.

**Red flags:** No firm equipment quote/vendor invoice, equipment with little
resale value as collateral.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "A loan just for a piece of equipment
— the equipment itself is the collateral."

**UNVERIFIED — confirm with 7FF** whether this is currently offered through
7FF's marketplace; it appears in the funding-director-orgo product catalog
as a draft/discovery-only card sourced from *other* companies' public sites
(Biz Funding Depot, Term Loan Solutions), not from 7FF directly.

## [INDUSTRY-STANDARD — not confirmed on 7FF's current public marketplace] Invoice / Receivables Factoring

**What it is:** Selling unpaid invoices at a discount for immediate cash.

**Who it fits:** B2B businesses with creditworthy customers and slow-paying
invoices.

**Red flags:** Few or no outstanding invoices, customers with poor payment
history, invoices already pledged elsewhere.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "Turning invoices you're waiting to
get paid on into cash today, for a fee."

**UNVERIFIED — confirm with 7FF** — same caveat as Equipment Financing above.

## [INDUSTRY-STANDARD — not confirmed on 7FF's current public marketplace] Business-Purpose Real Estate (DSCR / bridge / construction)

**What it is:** Financing structures for investment or business-use real
estate, often qualified on the property's cash flow (DSCR) rather than the
owner's personal income.

**Red flags:** Property doesn't cash-flow, unclear exit/refinance plan for a
bridge loan.

**One-sentence affiliate explanation:** "Real-estate financing that looks at
what the property earns, not just what the owner earns personally."

**UNVERIFIED — confirm with 7FF.**

## Personal/Business Credit Repair or Building — handle with care

The funding-director-orgo product catalog explicitly marks a
**credit-repair-referral** product as **`status: blocked`**, "pending
separate CROA-compliant program review" (CROA = Credit Repair Organizations
Act). This agent should **not** promote, sell, or route credit-repair
services as a 7FF offer. If an affiliate specializes in credit repair, treat
them as a *referral-source archetype* (see `04-affiliate-segments-and-sources.md`)
whose clients may later become 7FF funding prospects once their credit
profile qualifies — not as a product this agent pitches on 7FF's behalf.

General "business credit building" education (as opposed to consumer credit
repair) appears as a draft/discovery-only product family in the same
catalog, sourced from a third-party site, not from 7FF. Treat as
**UNVERIFIED — confirm with 7FF** whether 7FF offers this directly.
