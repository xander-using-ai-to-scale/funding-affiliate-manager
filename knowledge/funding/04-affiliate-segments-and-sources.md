# 04 — Affiliate Segments and Sources

Purpose: help the agent tailor tone, cadence, and offers by *who* the
affiliate is and *where* they came from, per the program-design-and-
segmentation approach (segment by audience fit, relationship strength,
reach, channel, prior activity, compliance risk, operating readiness).

Distinguish, for every affiliate: do they already have an audience/database
to mine (fast activation), or do they need to *generate* referral
opportunities first (slower activation, needs more enablement content)?

## Archetypes

### Coaches / consultants (business, sales, marketing coaches)
- **Motivation:** Adds a monetizable "capital" resource for clients without
  building lending expertise themselves; strengthens their own authority.
- **What resonates:** A simple one-pager or script they can hand a client
  ("if you need working capital, here's my guy"); social proof from other
  coaches; low time investment.
- **Watch for:** They may not want to be seen as "selling loans" — keep the
  framing as "resource I recommend," not "product I sell."

### Credit repair professionals
- **Motivation:** Their clients are actively rebuilding credit and are a
  natural funnel into funding once eligible. Adds a second revenue stream.
- **What resonates:** Clear guidance on *when* a client is fundable (so they
  don't waste referrals on clients who will get declined), and reassurance
  that 7FF's product is not itself a credit-repair service (see
  `02-funding-products.md` — credit repair is blocked/out of scope for this
  agent to sell).
- **Compliance note:** Because their own business is CROA-regulated, be extra
  careful not to blend 7FF funding claims with credit-repair claims in any
  joint messaging.

### CPAs / bookkeepers
- **Motivation:** Trusted advisor position; clients often ask them about
  financing decisions already. Referral is a natural extension of the
  relationship, plus incremental revenue.
- **What resonates:** Professional, low-hype framing; anything that sounds
  like a "loan pitch" will get rejected. Lead with client benefit and their
  own liability/scope boundaries (they are referring, not advising on the
  loan itself).
- **Watch for:** Highest compliance sensitivity of any segment — they may be
  bound by their own professional conduct rules about referral fees.
  Flag any CPA question about referral-fee disclosure rules for human
  review rather than answering definitively.

### Marketing agencies
- **Motivation:** Many already serve small-business clients who need
  capital to fund the marketing spend the agency is proposing. Referral fee
  is a bonus on top of the core service relationship.
- **What resonates:** Bundling language ("fund the campaign, then run it"),
  co-marketing assets, quick turnaround stories.

### Real-estate investors
- **Motivation:** Already fluent in leverage and capital stacking; may use
  0% card stacking or HELOC-type products for their own deals, and know
  other investors/business owners who need capital.
- **What resonates:** Speed, structure creativity (stacking multiple
  products), peer-to-peer credibility (investor talking to investor).

### Existing ISOs / brokers
- **Motivation:** Already monetizing funding referrals elsewhere; evaluating
  7FF as an additional or alternative funding source/marketplace.
- **What resonates:** Clarity on 7FF's marketplace breadth ("not just one
  lender"), the Partner CRM and Slack integration for tracking, and — once
  confirmed — actual commission economics (see `03-affiliate-economics.md`).
  This segment will ask sharp economics questions early; don't guess, route
  to a human with the specific question.

### Skool community members
- **Motivation:** Actively learning to build a "funding empire" (per the
  My Funding Machine Skool's stated purpose); may be earlier-stage than
  other segments and need more onboarding support.
- **What resonates:** Content that mirrors what they're learning in the
  classroom; live workshop tie-ins; peer wins from the same community.
- **UNVERIFIED — confirm with 7FF:** whether Skool members are automatically
  also 7FF partner-program affiliates, or are a separate audience this agent
  should route into the partner program first (see `01-company-and-offer.md`).

### LinkedIn connections
- **Motivation:** Varies widely — could be any of the above archetypes,
  contacted via a professional-network angle.
- **What resonates:** Short, non-salesy, tied to something specific and true
  about their profile/content (per the partner-sourcing skill's rule:
  outreach must be tied to a real audience or business need, never a
  fabricated relationship).

### Webinar / event attendees
- **Motivation:** Recently opted into learning more (masterclass, FM Live,
  a webinar) — warmer than cold outreach, but easy to lose if follow-up is
  slow.
- **What resonates:** Fast follow-up referencing the specific
  event/session they attended; low-friction next step (join Skool, get the
  partner link, etc.).

### Facebook (community/group members)
- **Motivation:** Similar to Skool — community-driven, likely earlier in
  the funnel.
- **What resonates:** Community-native tone, avoid anything that reads as a
  cold ad.

### Referral (affiliate referred another affiliate)
- **Motivation:** Trust transfer from an existing, presumably active
  affiliate.
- **What resonates:** Acknowledge the referring affiliate by name when
  appropriate/approved; fast-track onboarding since trust is pre-established.

### Legacy import (affiliates from a prior system/list)
- **Motivation:** Unknown/stale — could be anywhere from highly engaged to
  completely dead.
- **What resonates:** Treat as needing re-qualification before assuming
  activity level; see the dormant/reactivation segmentation in
  `05-conversation-playbook.md` (classify by prior production, engagement,
  audience fit, support need, and reason for inactivity — never assume the
  reason).
